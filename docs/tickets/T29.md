# T29: Every CI job has a time limit

[#78](https://github.com/nhandoanngoc-agilityio/trailer-to-campaign/issues/78) · PR [#79](https://github.com/nhandoanngoc-agilityio/trailer-to-campaign/pull/79) · draft PR, in review

Every job in every workflow now sets `timeout-minutes`, and a test fails if one doesn't, so a hung test costs at most 20 minutes of CI rather than GitHub's default of 6 hours.

## Spec

Found in the review of PR #76: no workflow set a limit, and pytest has no timeout of its own.

- **Done when:**
  - Every job in every file under `.github/workflows/` sets `timeout-minutes`: 20 for `test`, 5 for `answer-key`, and 30 for the Claude jobs.
  - A test fails if a job in any workflow has no `timeout-minutes`. It reads the files with PyYAML, declared as a dev dependency. (Changed by the human on 2026-11-05 from "adds no dependency", after a hand-written scanner proved unreliable in review. PyYAML was already installed through another package.)
  - A job that goes past its limit fails rather than passes. This rests on GitHub's documented behaviour; the record says it wasn't shown live.
- **Decisions:**
  - **The numbers:** the human approved 20, 5 and 30, sized from the issue's figure that the slowest of the last 31 PR runs of CI took 235 seconds. **Correction:** that figure is out of date, and the issue's text stays as it is. Run [41875685515](https://github.com/nhandoanngoc-agilityio/trailer-to-campaign/actions/runs/41875685515) later took 243 seconds, of which its `test` job took 240. Measured per job with `gh api` on 2026-11-05 at 02:40Z, and recounted at 03:12Z over the same window, every attempt of every `pull_request` run still listed, back to 2026-10-29:

    | Job | Runs with this job | Slowest passing | Note |
    |---|---|---|---|
    | `test` | 120 | 240 s | One attempt reached the limit at 1218 s and ended `cancelled` (see Evidence) |
    | `answer-key` | 72 | 10 s | T14 added it on 2026-10-30, partway through this window |
    | `claude-review` | 87 | 183 s | Some of these skipped the review, as on this PR, so this isn't a reliable worst case |
    | `claude` | 6 successful, not on `pull_request` | 119 s | Counted separately, see below |

    `claude` runs on comments and issues, so this was counted at 03:31Z over all its runs: 6 succeeded and 88 were skipped. The slowest job took 119 s ([run 41808056296](https://github.com/nhandoanngoc-agilityio/trailer-to-campaign/actions/runs/41808056296)), against its 30-minute limit. The slowest whole run took 683 s from `created_at` to `updated_at` ([run 41879859698](https://github.com/nhandoanngoc-agilityio/trailer-to-campaign/actions/runs/41879859698)), but its job took 76 s and ended ten minutes before that `updated_at`. Why isn't known, and the limit applies to the job. Six runs is a small sample.

    A job's time here runs from `started_at` to `completed_at`, so its wait in the queue isn't part of it. The longest wait was 15 s. 20 minutes is five times 240 seconds, so the agreed limits still fit. An earlier version of this record said `answer-key` was measured over 120 runs. That was wrong: only 72 of those runs have the job. Its 10 s is right.
  - **Which jobs are the Claude jobs:** `claude-review` in `claude-code-review.yml` and `claude` in `claude.yml`. The workflows hold exactly four jobs, and all four fit the agreed names.
  - **PyYAML reads the workflows, as a dev dependency:** the human's decision, on 2026-11-05. The ticket first said to add no dependency, so the test began as a hand-written scanner. Review found it unreliable in both directions, as the Review section shows: it passed shapes it shouldn't, and refused an ordinary workflow. PyYAML was already installed through another package, so declaring it adds no package. The approval covers PyYAML only.
  - **What a failure says:** each one names the file and the job, or the file alone if it has no jobs or isn't valid YAML. A job with no real limit is told to set `timeout-minutes` to a positive whole number, and shown what was found. A job that differs from `AGREED_LIMITS` is told to choose a limit on purpose.
  - **A new job needs a line in the test:** `AGREED_LIMITS` is compared exactly, so a new job or a renamed one fails until someone chooses its limit.
  - **A job that calls a reusable workflow is refused, with or without a limit.** [GitHub's reference](https://docs.github.com/en/actions/reference/workflows-and-actions/reusing-workflow-configurations) says "you can only use the following keywords in the job containing the call", and `timeout-minutes` isn't among them. There is no such job today. If one is added, the test stops and says a person must decide how to limit it. Whether GitHub rejects the keyword or ignores it wasn't tried.
  - **PyYAML isn't GitHub's parser:** it follows YAML 1.1. On a duplicate key the last one wins, `020` reads as 16, and merge keys work. Whether GitHub reads these the same way wasn't checked. None appears in these workflows, and a job read as a different number fails the exact comparison.
  - **Not covered:** the limit is on the whole job, so a hang still costs the full 20 minutes and pytest doesn't name the test that hung.
  - **For the human: Done-when 3.** A job past its limit ends `cancelled`, not `failure`, as the Evidence shows. Whether that meets "fails rather than passes" is the human's decision, and the issue's wording is unchanged.
  - **For the human: the `ci.yml:39` comment.** It says the Answer Key guard "shows red". A timed-out `answer-key` would show `cancelled`, going by the `test` job's behaviour. That wasn't tested on `answer-key`, and the comment is unchanged.

- **Files:** one `timeout-minutes` line in each of the four jobs, across `ci.yml`, `claude-code-review.yml` and `claude.yml`. Permissions, triggers and secrets are unchanged.

**What changed from the plan:** the plan said no dependency. After review round 2, the human chose PyYAML and changed the Done-when. So `pyproject.toml` and `uv.lock` are now part of this PR, and the scanner is gone. The workflow files didn't change in any review round.

## Tests

`tests/test_workflows.py` has 58 tests in 304 lines. The scanner's last version had 79 in 432 lines. The code that reads and compares the limits is 24 lines, against about 10 hoped for, because each kept rule needs its own check and message. The full suite had 1163 passing at the time of the evidence below.

- **The real workflows (1):** every job has exactly its agreed limit.
- **A copy of the real workflows (7):** an untouched copy passes, and a new job, a new file, a renamed job, a changed limit, a removed file and a removed limit each fail.
- **A valid workflow passes (5):** limits on every job, an anchored and aliased limit, JSON and script in a step's block text (the round 2 bug), a step's `uses:`, and `.yml` and `.yaml` read with other files ignored.
- **A limit that isn't a positive whole number is rejected (13):** quoted, zero, negative, `true`, `yes`, `false`, a fraction, `5.0`, blank, null, an expression, a list and a mapping.
- **A job with no limit of its own is rejected (15):** none, in a comment (two forms), on a step, under another key, inside block text, inside an open brace, nested braces or a multi-line quote, at the workflow level, under another top-level key, on the job before it, and a job that's empty, block text or a number.
- **A job that calls a reusable workflow is refused (3):** with no limit, with a limit, and with a quoted key.
- **The gate can't pass by finding nothing (14):** an empty directory, a directory with no workflow files, eight shapes of file with no jobs, a bad file after a good one, invalid YAML, two documents in one file, and a Python tag that only an unsafe loader reads.

## Close the loop

- **Glossary:** no new terms.
- **ADR:** none. The limits are one-line changes, easy to reverse. Declaring PyYAML is a dev dependency that adds no package, and it's recorded in Spec.
- **Lesson:** a rule against dependencies has a price, so check what it's costing, and a limit's outcome may not carry the name you wrote down for it. See `docs/lessons.md`.
